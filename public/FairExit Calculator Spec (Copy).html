1 · Overview

1. User answersRegion, age, years, pay, role, and a few taps.
2. Map answers to inputsEach answer becomes a number or flag (section 2).
3. Call the engineFairExitRules.estimate(region, inputs)
4. Show the resultEstimate range, the 3 parts, a message, a source link.
Key points
  1. Canada shows dollars. A range from the legal minimum up to what courts may award.
  2. US shows no dollars. It's a checklist of federal and state rules that may apply (section 7).
  3. The engine is pure JavaScript with no dependencies. It runs the same in the browser or on a Node backend. You can call it as-is or port the tables below to your language.
  4. Every result carries the label "Rough estimate from public rules, not legal advice."

3 · Canada: how the estimate is calculated

The result has three parts. Weekly pay = salary ÷ 52. Monthly pay = salary ÷ 12.

Step 1 · Notice pay (legal minimum)

Look up the province's table (section 4) by years of service. Use the highest row the person qualifies for. Example, Ontario: 8 years → 8 weeks.

Step 2 · Severance pay (only Ontario and federal)

  1. Ontario: 1 week per year of service, prorated, max 26 weeks. Only if 5+ years AND payroll is Yes (or 50+ let go in 6 months from a closure). Payroll "Not sure" → show "may apply, up to X weeks", never $0.
  2. Federal: the greater of 2 days per full year or 5 days, after 1 year. Days ÷ 5 = weeks.
  3. All other provinces: none.

Step 3 · Group layoff (raises the minimum)

If groupLayoff and the province has group rules, look up group weeks by groupSize. The person gets the higher of individual and group notice (BC adds them). Some provinces show it as "may apply" only. Table in section 4.

Legal minimum ($) = (notice weeks + severance weeks) × weekly pay.

Step 4 · What courts may award (the top of the range)

months = years × 1.0
if age ≥ 55: months + 3   else if age ≥ 50: + 2   else if age ≥ 45: + 1
months = months × roleFactor
months = min(months, 24)
low  = max(1, round(months × 0.80))
high = min(24, round(months × 1.15))
then × market factor (0.90 / 1.00 / 1.15), rounded
$ = months × monthly pay, never below the legal minimum
Fix needed (found while writing this): the market factor is applied after the 24-month cap, so "Very few jobs" can push it to 28 months (example D). Cap at 24 again after the market factor.

Step 5 · The range shown

  1. Low end = legal minimum $.
  2. High end = court-award high $.
  3. Exception: if the letter limits them to the minimum (Q10 = Yes) AND the contract is for the same role (Q11), show only the legal minimum and grey out part 3 with: "may not apply — your answers say a valid clause caps you".

7 · US: what the free check shows (no dollars)

The US has no legal minimum severance, so the US free check shows no dollar amount. It lists the federal and state rules the person's answers point to, with an official source link.

Questions

State · age 40+ · how it ended (laid off / site closed / big group / for cause) · employer size (under 100 / 100+ / not sure) · how many let go (just me / a handful / 50+ / not sure) · notice given (none / 1–2 wks / 3–8 wks / 8+ wks) · release to sign · severance promised in writing · time to decide (under 21 / 21–44 / 45+ days) · 7-day take-back · union · non-compete · anything else.

8 · Build notes

  1. Simplest path: load severance-rules.js on the server (Node) or in the browser, and call FairExitRules.estimate(region, inputs). Read statutory, commonLaw, massWarning from the result.
  2. Apply the market factor and the clause rule (section 3, steps 4–5) after the call.
  3. Run node severance-rules.test.js (83 tests) plus examples A–H above to check your build.
  4. Save the inputs and result with the user's session so the paid report can reuse them.
  5. All numbers are pending employment-lawyer sign-off before launch. The tables won't change shape, only values if a lawyer corrects them.
